How the 6 g enclosure holds together

The board is neither screwed nor glued in. It tucks under two lips in the lower shell, drops onto four corner rests, and a hook bent back by hand springs over its edge. The cover slides down the sensor flex and snaps on with four clips. In flight the whole 20 g probe hangs from the ring on the lower shell, so the same 0.6 mm part that holds the board carries the load.

The sequence

  1. The hook at the ring end is bent back with a fingernail or a tool. The board's far end tucks under two fixed lips, the board drops onto four corner rests, and the hook springs back over its edge. No screws, no adhesive.

  2. The sensor flex plugs straight up into its connector, and the AAA cell clips into the two holders on the board.

  3. The flex's sensor end is fed out through the slot in the cover, and the cover slides down the flex. At each of the four clips, a slim bar above a window in the lower shell flexes inward over the cover's barb and springs back above it.

  4. The probe hangs from the balloon's thread by the ring on the lower shell. The flex leaves the cover square to its face and sags under its own weight in an even arc, out in free air, away from the electronics.

  5. Turned about the thread, the floor of the lower shell shows the power button, a disc in the 0.6 mm wall that is pressed onto the board's tact switch, and the window for the status LED.

  6. Everything, 20 g in all, hangs from the ring. The board and cell rest on the lower shell's far end, so the lower shell carries their weight up to the ring; the cover hangs on its four clips.

About this animation

  • Built from the FD-11 1.G CAD: the flight shells (March 2026) and the board assembly. The small hole for the EMC test cable, which only the test shells have, is left out.
  • The hook and clip deflections come from the interference in the CAD: the hook's top moves about 2 mm so its lip clears the board, and each clip bar moves 0.75 mm to pass the barb. The bending shapes are drawn, not computed.
  • The arrows show where the load goes, not how it is distributed; no FEA was run on this part.
  • To open the shell, the tabs below the clip windows are squeezed one side at a time, which pulls the bars off the barbs.
