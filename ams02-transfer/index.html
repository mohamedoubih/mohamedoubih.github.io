03 · AMS-02 Layer 0 integration tooling

From one ladder to Layer 0

First the carbon-composite QLCS is located on Station 2. Then each silicon-sensor ladder, which has to land within about ±100 µm of its place and 200 µm from its neighbour, is aligned once at Station 1; the vacuum positioning jig carries that alignment, without letting go, to the bond at Station 2. Finally the full quadrant is glued onto the Layer 0 plane at Station 3, which goes on top of AMS-02.

A · The QLCS on Station 2

  1. Thirteen PEEK support buttons are glued in their pockets, standing 0.5 mm proud of the plate; twelve of them sit under this QLCS. Two guide pins are already in the station.

  2. The QLCS goes down 3 mm off its final position: the guide pins enter its two diagonal slots and the button caps pass through the holes in its bottom skin. The precision hole is still covered.

  3. The panel slides 3 mm at 45°, the only move the diagonal slots allow. Its bottom skin slides under the button caps, which flex and press it onto the bases, so the QLCS is held in z as well. The precision hole opens.

  4. Pins go into the precision hole and the horizontal slot, fixing the QLCS in x–y without over-constraining it, and the guide pins come out. The buttons hold it 0.5 mm above the plate.

  5. Ladders 1–4 are bonded row by row, each with the sequence shown next for ladder 5.

B · Ladder 5, Station 1 to Station 2

  1. The ladder arrives in its transport container. The cover and the fixing brackets are removed.

  2. The ladder is moved to the central zone and pushed against the precision pins, which fix its x–y position on the station.

  3. The jig carries a ball-ended pin at each end (a Ø5 mm pin with a Ø8 mm spherical head). It comes down electronics end first, so that pin finds the precision hole before the far pin drops into the slot. Vacuum pads then hold every sensor; nothing clamps the silicon.

  4. The jig lifts the ladder, turns over and sets down in the glue zone with the bonding face up.

  5. A mask is laid on the ladder, adhesive is applied through it, and the mask is lifted off.

  6. The jig turns back over and is carried by its handles to Station 2. The ladder stays on the jig the whole way, so its datum goes with it.

  7. Electronics end first again: the round pin enters the precision hole for row 5, then the far end comes down into the slot until the feet seat on the plate. The ladder lands 200 µm from ladder 4 on a 0.1 mm bond line.

  8. The adhesive cures over several days with the jig in place, holding the bonded geometry.

  9. Once the adhesive has cured, the vacuum is released and the jig lifted away. The ladder stays bonded to the QLCS.

C · The quadrant onto Layer 0

  1. Ladders 6–9 go on with the 10- and 8-sensor jigs, completing the nine rows of the quadrant.

  2. The transport jig is located on Station 2 by its pins and holds the populated QLCS by suction on the sensors, keeping it flat. The fixing pins come out and the quadrant is lifted.

  3. Station 3 holds the octagonal L0 support plane, with support buttons glued in its pockets as on Station 2. 3M 2216 epoxy is spread with a spatula through a 0.6 mm mask with Ø10 mm holes, and the mask comes off.

  4. As on Station 2, the QLCS goes down 3 mm off position, slides 3 mm at 45° under the button caps and is pinned. The glue discs spread but are spaced so they never merge and trap air.

  5. The glue, which carries the transport and launch loads, cures. The suction is released and the transport jig lifted away.

  6. The other three quadrants go on the same way: QLCS-R and QLCS-L alternate, rotated 45° on the zenith plane. (The nadir plane underneath is not shown.)

  7. The upper and lower cupolas close the plane: carbon-skin sandwich covers that protect against micrometeorites, shield it thermally and electrically, and keep it light-tight.

  8. Layer 0 sits on top of AMS-02, above Layer 1, adding a new measurement plane to the silicon tracker.

About this animation

  • A simplified model built from the thesis CAD: Station 1, the L12 positioning jig, and Station 2 with a QLCS-L panel. Dimensions are approximate.
  • The thin layers (ladder 0.52 mm, adhesive 0.10 mm, button stand-off 0.5 mm) are drawn thicker so they show up; the insets give the real arrangement.
  • Ladders 1–4 are placed in a time-lapse and ladder 5 is shown in full. The cure, which takes several days, is compressed.
  • Station 3 follows the procedure in thesis chapter 4, where the button engagement and the glue layer were tried on a prototype. AMS-02 is heavily simplified.
