01 · Miniature pressure-relief valve

How the valve cracks

Balloon gas acts on a large Kapton diaphragm; a spring pushes back through the plunger, which holds a steel ball on a PTFE seat. When the pressure force overcomes the spring preload, the ball lifts and the balloon vents. Turning the preload screw sets that pressure.

The sequence

  1. Lower housing, seat O-ring, PTFE seat and steel ball; the plunger and Kapton diaphragm; spring seat, spring and jam nut; the upper housing, clamped by six M3 bolts; and the M6 preload screw.

  2. Cut through the axis and the inlet barb. Red is balloon gas at inlet pressure, blue is ambient.

  3. The spring pushes down on the spring seat, diaphragm and plunger, and the plunger holds the 3.175 mm steel ball on the machined PTFE seat. The diaphragm collects the pressure; the ball does the sealing.

  4. Gas from the balloon fills the chamber under the diaphragm. The opening force ΔP·Aeff grows, but while it is below the spring force kx the ball stays on its seat.

  5. At ΔP·Aeff = kx the diaphragm and plunger lift against the spring, the ball comes off the seat, and gas flows through the seat to the outlet.

  6. Venting lowers the pressure; once the opening force drops below the spring force, the spring returns the ball to the seat and the valve seals again.

  7. Turning the preload screw changes x, and the cracking pressure follows it linearly: 1.4–6.5 kPa measured across the preload range, about 450 Pa per millimetre.

About this animation

  • Built from the Valve 5F CATIA assembly. The spring is drawn for illustration with the real rate (0.27 N/mm); its coil dimensions are approximate.
  • The lift of the plunger and ball is exaggerated so the gap at the seat can be seen, and the 55 µm diaphragm is drawn thicker.
  • The cycle uses a 5.5 mm preload. The force balance gives about 2.5 kPa; the valve measured 2.37 kPa at that setting, the difference being a small fitted offset force.
